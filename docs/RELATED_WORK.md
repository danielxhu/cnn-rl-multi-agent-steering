# Related work and novelty check (20 Nov 2026)

## Status and limits of this check

This is a quick check, not a systematic review.

- **How it was done.** Four parallel searches, one per research question, with 6–12 queries each.
- **How the citations were checked.** Each work was confirmed from its arXiv, DOI, venue or project page (title, authors, year, venue). Full texts were mostly not read.
- **Unverified details.** Cells marked *(recall)* describe a paper's design from memory and must be checked before citing.
- **No citation tracking.** Google Scholar and dblp block automated fetches, so "cited by" searches were not run.

## Research questions

| RQ | Question |
|---|---|
| RQ1 | Has anyone built the chain *photo of a drawing → simulatable scene → learned multi-agent steering* end to end? |
| RQ2 | What is the closest work on decentralised RL collision avoidance with ray observations, and what does our setup add? |
| RQ3 | What is the closest work on agent-built spatial memory for RL navigation, and how does E7 differ? |
| RQ4 | Has a learned steering policy been compared head to head with symbolic, GA-discovered steering models (E6)? |

## RQ1: drawing → scene → learned multi-agent steering

| Work | What it does | Difference from ours |
|---|---|---|
| Montana Gonzalez & Maddock, J. Virtual Reality & Broadcasting 16(3), 2021 ([link](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/174780)) | Users sketch entrances, exits, barriers and flow lines on screen; the navmesh updates live | Closest in what the sketch contains, but it is interactive on screen (no photo parsing) and the agents follow rules, not a learned policy |
| Mathew, Benes, Aliaga, CGF 41(8) / SCA 2022 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.14629)) | Sketch gestures set crowd paths, speed and density | The sketch describes motion, not a scene; hand-made crowd model |
| Hassaan et al., Sensors 23(19), 2023 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/s23198344)) | Parses photos of emergency floor plans into escape routes | Closest on the perception side; no agents and no learning after it |
| Berggold et al., arXiv 2306.15318, 2023 | A ViT predicts evacuation time and density directly from a floor plan | Replaces the simulator with a learned surrogate; no agents |
| Xu et al., Sketch2Scene, arXiv 2408.04567, 2024 | Sketch → 3D game scene | Builds scenes only; no agents are trained |
| Loo & Hsu, Scene Action Maps, ICRA 2024 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07948)) | Parses sketches and floor plans into maps a single robot plans with | The map is used for planning, not as a multi-agent training environment |
| Xu et al., SkeNa, arXiv 2508.03053, 2025 | A hand-drawn map guides one agent through a real 3D scene | The sketch is a hint, not the environment itself |
| Deitke et al., Phone2Proc, CVPR 2023 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04819)) | Phone scan → procedural simulation → RL → real robot | Closest *pipeline*: but the input is a 3D scan, not a drawing, and it is single-agent |
| Chhablani et al., EmbodiedSplat, ICCV 2025 | Phone capture → Gaussian splats → policy training | Real scans, single agent |
| Deitke et al., ProcTHOR, NeurIPS 2022 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06994)) | Procedurally generated houses for training embodied AI | Generated by a program, not drawn by a user |

**Answer.** No work found combines all three parts. Each part on its own has close prior work:

- sketched scenes with exits exist, but their agents follow rules;
- phone capture → simulator → RL exists, but for a single agent starting from a 3D scan;
- RL crowds exist, but on hand-built scenes.

The likeliest reviewer objections are Montana Gonzalez & Maddock (2021) and Phone2Proc (2023).

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Kwiatkowski et al., MIG 2023 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12841)) | Reward design | None *(recall)* | – | Studies reward, not observation |
| Panayiotou et al., CCP, SIGGRAPH 2022 | Behaviour weights as policy inputs | – | – | Studies behaviour control |

Surveys: Mavrogiannis et al., ACM THRI 2023 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05668)); Alyassi et al., Frontiers in Robotics and AI, 2025. No survey dedicated to RL crowd simulation was found.

**Answer.** The core setup is **not new**:

- a shared PPO policy on ego-centric rays, compared with ORCA (Long 2018, Fan 2020);
- navigation without a map (Tai 2017).

These look new **as a combination**, but no paper was found that rules them out:

- rays read separately for three entity classes (obstacle, wall, agent);
- a goal **bearing only**, with no distance, map or position;
- a GRU as temporal memory, tested on purpose on layout families with dead ends;
- a two-sided ORCA comparison: clearly beats map-free ORCA, ties map-guided ORCA.

Frame this part as an empirical study, not as a new method.

**Open risk.** One search summary described a multi-agent lidar + LSTM policy by "Diao & Wang", but its title and venue could not be confirmed. Search for it before claiming anything about memory.

## RQ3: agent-built spatial memory (E7)

| Work | Memory | How it reaches the policy | Multi-agent | Difference from E7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gupta et al., CMP, CVPR 2017 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03920)) | Learned top-down belief map | A differentiable value-iteration planner chooses the action | No | Map → planner → action, all learned. E7 uses a fixed planner whose output is an *input* to the policy |
| Chaplot et al., Active Neural SLAM, ICLR 2020 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05155)) | Occupancy map plus learned pose correction | Analytic planner → short-term goal → RL local policy | No | **Closest prior idea.** E7 has no learned SLAM, gives a direction (2 numbers) instead of a subgoal, uses a virtual goal along the bearing, and runs many agents with one shared policy |
| Parisotto & Salakhutdinov, Neural Map, ICLR 2018 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.08360)) | Learned 2-D feature map | The network reads the whole map | No | Learned memory; much wider input to the policy |
| Beeching et al., EgoMap, ECML-PKDD 2020 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02286)) | Feature map in an egocentric frame | The network reads it | No | Learned features, not geometry |
| Savinov et al., SPTM, ICLR 2018 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.00653)) | Topological graph | Graph search picks a waypoint image | No | Needs a prior exploration video |
| Mirowski et al., ICLR 2017 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03673)) | LSTM plus auxiliary tasks | Implicit, in the recurrent state | No | Recurrent memory only; matches our finding that a longer BPTT window alone does not help |
| Wayne et al., MERLIN, arXiv 2018 | Learned external memory | Read by the network | No | Not spatial |
| Faust et al., PRM-RL, ICRA 2018; Francis et al., T-RO 2020 | Roadmap on a known map | RL follows the waypoints; tested with sensor and motion noise | No | Needs the full map beforehand |
| de Heuvel et al., arXiv 2303.01443, 2023 | A* path on known static obstacles | The path is an RL input | No | Planner hint as RL input, but the map is known |
| Yu et al., MAANS, arXiv 2110.05734 | Per-agent maps, merged | A learned planner chooses goals | **Yes** | Maps are shared and the planner is learned |
| D2M2N, arXiv 2310.07070 | Compressed latent memory per robot | Used by the network | **Yes** | Learned latent memory |

**Answer.** E7 is **incremental engineering over known ideas**, not a new idea. These are already known:

- building a map online and planning on it;
- feeding a planner's output to an RL policy;
- robustness to odometry noise;
- multi-agent mapping.

What may be ours:

- a very small interface: 2 numbers, not a map or a subgoal;
- a virtual goal far along a bearing-only target;
- a cheap map built only from each agent's own ray hits, private to that agent, with one policy shared by all;
- the control result: a 4× longer BPTT window gives nothing, while the explicit map gives +3.3 points of success and about one third fewer loops.

Suggested framing: *"a minimal-bandwidth planner hint, not longer recurrent memory, is what fixes looping under bearing-only goals."* Cite Active Neural SLAM and CMP as the nearest work.

**Risk.** A reviewer may call this "Active Neural SLAM without the learning". The gain is modest, so it needs confidence intervals over more seeds.

## RQ4: learned vs symbolic steering (E6)

| Work | What | Difference from E6 |
|---|---|---|
| Le & Hu, WSC 2020 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/WSC48552.2020.9384033)) | Model-space specification for mobile agents | The framework E6 would use as its symbolic baseline |
| Le & Hu, ANNSIM 2022 ([dblp](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/annsim/LeH22)) | GA discovery of steering models | Symbolic only |
| Le & Hu, *Simulation* 101(7), 2025 ([dblp](https://dblp.dagstuhl.de/rec/journals/simulation/LeH25.html)) | Journal follow-up: leader following, personal space, avoiding moving obstacles | Most likely source of E6's scenarios and metrics; no RL. **Not yet cited in our README** |
| Zhong et al., Applied Soft Computing 2017 ([gpbib](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Zhong_2017_ASC.html)); AAMAS 2014 | Gene expression programming learns crowd rules from video | Fitted to data, no RL comparison |
| Hein et al., Eng. Appl. AI 2018 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04170)) | GP policies vs GP imitation of a black-box RL policy | Control benchmarks, not steering |
| Roth et al., MSVIPER, arXiv 2209.09079; XAI-N, arXiv 2104.10818 | RL navigation policy distilled into a decision tree | One robot; no discovered-model baseline |
| Bastani et al., VIPER, NeurIPS 2018; Verma et al., PIRL, ICML 2018 | Policy → tree / program | General methods. Cited from memory; open the pages before citing |

**Answer.** No head-to-head comparison of a PPO steering policy, GA-discovered symbolic models and a distilled tree on the same scenarios and metrics was found. Every pairing of two of the three exists; the three-way comparison does not. E6 looks like the clearest novelty, but the "cited by" search on Le & Hu 2022 and 2025 is still to be done.

## Verdict

| Contribution | Status |
|---|---|
| Shared PPO policy on ray observations, compared with ORCA | Not new (Long 2018, Fan 2020) |
| Bearing-only goal, 3-class rays, GRU tested on dead ends | New as a combination; frame as an empirical study. Check "Diao & Wang" first |
| E7 wall memory → 2-number planner hint | Incremental; nearest work is Active Neural SLAM and CMP. The BPTT control is the strongest evidence |
| Photo of a drawing → scene → learned multi-agent steering | **Not found anywhere**; the integration is new. Nearest: Montana Gonzalez & Maddock 2021, Phone2Proc 2023 |
| E6: learned vs GA-discovered vs distilled tree | **Not found**; likely the strongest novelty, and the advisor's own framework is the baseline |
